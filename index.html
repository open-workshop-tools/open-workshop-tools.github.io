01 / Why we exist

A workshop should be full of tools that just work, year after year.

Nobody asks you to sign in before you pick up a ruler. A square does not stop working when a card payment fails. Yet much of today's design software works exactly like that: programs became services, licences became subscriptions, and drawings moved to servers that belong to someone else.

For large companies that may be a fair trade. For students, makers with a 3D printer or a small CNC, repair shops, small workshops and hobby engineers, it often means renting the right to open their own work.

So we build a family of focused tools instead of one giant program. Each does one job well, with its own name and its own releases. They share the same principles and, where it helps, the same core. Where a great open-source tool already exists, we connect to it instead of rebuilding it.

Free tools that work offline, read and write open formats, and can never be taken away, because their source code belongs to everyone.

02 / Principles

Six promises every tool keeps.

01

Free, forever

No price, no trial, no "pro" tier. Commercial use included.

02

No account, no cloud

Nothing to sign in to and no network access. Files go where you save them.

03

Open formats

Project files you can read in a text editor. Exports in STEP, STL, DXF and other standards.

04

Open source

MIT licensed and public on GitHub. Anyone can check it, fix it or carry it on.

05

Private by design

No telemetry, no tracking. What you design stays on your machine.

06

Honest scope

Each tool says plainly what it can and cannot do yet. Small and solid beats big and broken.

04 / Support us

Like the idea? Give it a star.

We do not ask for money, and we never will. The one thing that helps a small open-source project the most costs you nothing and takes two seconds: a star on GitHub.

Why a star matters

  • People find us. GitHub shows starred projects higher in search and in its trending lists, so more makers discover free tools.
  • It shows the project is alive. New users trust a project that others already use.
  • It tells us what to build. Stars on each tool show where the next hours of work are best spent.
  • It keeps us going. Every star is a small "thank you" to the people writing the code.

How to star, in three steps

  1. Sign in to GitHub, or create a free account.
  2. Open the project: github.com/open-workshop-tools/Open-Caliper
  3. Click ★ Star in the top right corner. Done!
